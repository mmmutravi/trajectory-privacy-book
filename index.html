01 / ABOUT THE BOOK

Making mobility data
useful and private.

The edited volume explores the balance between data utility and privacy in pervasive, mobile, and location-aware environments. It brings together research on trajectory data, location-based services (LBS), mobility analytics, privacy threats, re-identification risks, and trustworthy processing of spatiotemporal information. The book addresses privacy models, privacy-preserving techniques, secure trajectory processing, and intelligent approaches that enable meaningful analysis while protecting sensitive movement patterns.

It also examines AI, machine learning, IoT, blockchain, and distributed technologies for secure location-based systems, with applications in smart cities, intelligent transportation, healthcare, emergency services, tourism, and logistics.

05 / FOR CONTRIBUTORS

Submission guidelines for authors

Prepare a focused proposal that clearly explains the research contribution and its relevance to the volume.

A

Proposal requirements

  • Submit a chapter proposal / extended abstract of 500–800 words outlining objectives, scope, approach, and contributions.
  • After proposal acceptance, invited authors will receive full-chapter instructions, including length, abstract, keywords, formatting, and references.
  • Original research, review articles, case studies, technical frameworks, and application-oriented studies are welcome.
  • Submissions must be original and not under consideration elsewhere.
B

Types of contributions

The editors welcome theoretical foundations, novel algorithms, system architectures, comparative studies, experimental evaluations, implementation reports, case studies, and comprehensive surveys. Contributions should clearly identify the research problem, explain the proposed approach, and discuss its relevance to privacy, data utility, security, or intelligent location-based services.

C

Who should contribute?

Researchers, faculty members, industry practitioners, and doctoral scholars working in pervasive computing, mobility data analytics, location privacy, spatial databases, blockchain, artificial intelligence, IoT, cybersecurity, and location-based applications are encouraged to submit chapter proposals.

D

Important notes

  • Authors must ensure originality and comply with publication ethics.
  • Permission is required for third-party figures, tables, datasets, and copyrighted material.
  • Publisher requirements concerning review, similarity screening, AI-assisted tools, charges, royalties, and contributor agreements will apply.
E

What a chapter proposal should cover

Each proposal should make clear: (i) the problem being addressed and its significance; (ii) the research objectives and questions; (iii) the proposed method, framework, or review approach; (iv) the main expected contributions; and (v) how the chapter advances privacy-preserving trajectory intelligence or trustworthy location-based systems.

Authors are encouraged to explain the relationship between privacy protection and data utility, identify the assumptions and limitations of their approach, and discuss practical relevance or open research challenges where appropriate.

F

Publisher & target audience

Publisher: Cambridge Scholars Publishing, United Kingdom. The volume addresses an interdisciplinary readership and brings together research in privacy-preserving computing, location-based systems, pervasive computing, AI, IoT, blockchain, and mobility analytics.

Target audience: Researchers, academicians, doctoral scholars, postgraduate students, data scientists, and professionals in location-based services, pervasive computing, cybersecurity, AI/ML, IoT, mobile computing, smart cities, intelligent transportation, and spatiotemporal data management.
